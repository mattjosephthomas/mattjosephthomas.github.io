Most tourism businesses I meet are good. The place is beautiful, the people care, the product works. And they're still stuck with a few strong months, too many hours, too much admin and not enough profit.

I learned why running Parkbus. The buses were never the point. The point was the system around them: how people find out, how they book, how they get there, and who else has to say yes. Change the system and the same product makes more money. Over the past two years we've grown Parkbus about 30% a year, with a very small team, running routes coast to coast.

I also co-founded Whereabouts, software that helps destination organizations work with their local businesses. It started as my thesis project, and it's still going: more than 30 destinations across North America use it.

The pattern was the same both times. I find the bottleneck, design a better system, and bring the right people together to build it. So that's what I do now: for the companies I run, the companies I invest in, and the occasional business that just needs a good conversation.

How I work

Four steps, every time. Whether I'm running the company, investing in it or advising it.

  1. Diagnose

    Where does this business leave money on the table? I look for the bottlenecks: the empty seats, the slow replies, the products priced too low, the work that eats the owner's week.

  2. Design

    I draw the better system. Who the customer is, what they buy, how they find it, how it gets delivered, and what it should cost.

  3. Assemble

    I bring the right people and the right partners together. The team, the tourism board, the park, the rail line, the supplier. Most good ideas stall because the right people never sat at one table.

  4. Improve

    Then I stay with it. Small fixes, month after month, until the gains compound.

The goal is simple: more profit, less scramble, and a business that doesn't depend on the owner doing everything.

Visit Canada Parks

The fun way to experience Canada's parks

Official park sites are good at telling you the rules. Visit Canada Parks tells you what you can do: the best lookouts, the best time to go, where to jump into cold clear water, where the leaves are turning right now. It's also a way for destinations near a park to put their hotels, restaurants and guides in front of people who are already planning a trip.

Nature Hack

A hike is better with the right person leading it, and that person isn't always a biologist. Nature Hack puts storytellers, comedians and performers on the trail so a day outside feels like a good night out. Small groups, a bus from the city, and a guide people want to spend the day with.
